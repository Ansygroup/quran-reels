Posting for the Hijri New Year | Quran Reels

The Hijri new year calls for reflection, not fireworks: correct dates first (confirm against the calendar — moon sighting differences vary by country), then content that serves the moment: gratitude, audit, and intention. Hijri new year covers the three angles and the claims that should never appear.

  • Confirm the date for your audience's context before publishing.
  • Three angles: gratitude for the passed year, honest audit, one intention.
  • No invented virtues for the date — publish only what your sources verify.

The three angles

Gratitude: one verse or reminder about counting favors — short, warm, shareable. Audit: three questions for the viewer (what was kept, what slipped, what to repair) presented as reflection, not as a checklist sermon.

Intention: one written intention with a start date — specific enough to act on tomorrow. Together the three make a series of three clips or one longer video.

Accuracy discipline

State the date as observed in your audience's region, or say "varies by moon sighting" if you serve several countries. Wrong dates erode trust faster than any stylistic flaw.

Claims about specific virtues of the first day belong to scholars and their sources; if you cannot cite it properly, leave it out. A correct, modest post outperforms an ambitious, unverifiable one.

FAQ

Is greeting on this day allowed?

Greetings of ordinary courtesy are fine for many scholars; avoid treating it like the two Eids in ritual or greeting formulas without scholarly grounding.

What if my country's date differs?

Acknowledge both where relevant, or follow your audience's majority context with a short note.

How long should the post be?

Thirty to sixty seconds per angle; the reflection format rewards brevity.
