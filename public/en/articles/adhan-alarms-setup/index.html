Adhan Alarms and Prayer Reminders That Actually Wake You | Quran Reels

An alarm you can silence while asleep is not a reminder — it is decoration. Prayer alarms work when they follow three rules: one margin before the true time, a sound you respect, and a phone position that forces you to stand. Prayer alarms shows the exact times; the alarm setup turns times into action.

  • One alarm a few minutes before the prayer, not at its end.
  • A sound you recognize as serious — not the gentlest tone you own.
  • Phone placed out of arm's reach of the pillow.

Build the alarm set

Create one alarm per prayer with a margin you trust (two to five minutes before the adhan, depending on your household). Name each alarm after the prayer so your half-asleep self knows what it is for.

Use different sounds for Fajr and the rest: the ear tags novelty, and a unique Fajr sound survives deeper sleep than the tone you hear three more times that day.

Keep the times honest

Once a week — pick Sunday night — open the prayer-times page and confirm every alarm still matches the current times. Seasonal shifts of a few minutes accumulate silently until an alarm stops meaning anything.

When you silence an alarm, stand up in the same motion. Training your body to associate the sound with standing is worth more than any louder tone.

Start today

Open Prayer alarms now, set today's five alarms with margins, and place the phone charger across the room. Run the weekly check every Sunday until it becomes automatic.

FAQ

How many minutes before the prayer?

Enough to finish what you are doing and reach the prayer place calmly — usually two to five minutes.

Is an adhan sound allowed as an alarm?

Yes for many scholars when used as a reminder; if you prefer, use a neutral tone and let the adhan itself sound at the true time.

What about DST changes?

That is exactly what the weekly check is for — confirm times after every clock change.
