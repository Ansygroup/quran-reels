Prayer Times Accuracy:13 Calculation Methods Explained | Quran Reels

The gap between apps is not a bug — it is fifteen different scholarly methods computing twilight differently. Prayer times exposes thirteen of them with timezone and DST handling, so the fix is configuration: choose the method your local authority follows and the times line up.

  • Choose the calculation method your country's authority follows.
  • Enable automatic DST where applicable so times move with the clock.
  • After travel, re-check the city first — settings are location-bound.

Why two apps disagree by several minutes

Methods differ in the sun angles they use for Fajr and Isha. Each is valid in some jurisdiction; none is a math error. Comparing apps without comparing methods only produces anxiety.

When an app and our page disagree, check three things in order: method, city, and DST setting. The misconfiguration is almost always one of those three.

Travel and the daily rhythm

On a trip, open the page once on arrival and confirm the city. A stale saved location is the classic source of 'why was my alarm ten minutes late'.

For daily use, set alarms from the same source you read — mixing an app's table with another app's notifications recreates the discrepancy you were trying to avoid.

Start today

Open Prayer times now, set your city and the method your authority uses, and compare tomorrow's Fajr with the official table. If they match, you are configured; if not, change the method, not the app.

FAQ

Which method is 'correct'?

The one your local authority follows. The page presents thirteen documented methods precisely because correctness here is jurisdictional.

Do times update offline?

Once the page is open, today's times remain available; a fresh load refreshes them for your current location.

Why did my alarm fire late after travel?

Usually a cached city. Open the page and confirm location whenever you move — it takes seconds.
